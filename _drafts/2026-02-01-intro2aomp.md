### What is AoMP?

*The Art of MRI Physics (AoMP)* is a precise and carefully crafted distillation of the author's more than 15 years of experience in MRI physics, signal processing, and mathematics. It is designed to be accessible to readers of all backgrounds. The series covers foundational concepts in mathematics, physics, signal processing, and MRI physics, with an emphasis on intuitive understanding without sacrificing rigor. 

MRI is a life-saving technology that has revolutionized medical imaging and diagnostics, grounded in fascinating physics and mathematics. However, it is often perceived as complex and inaccessible to those without a technical background. The AoMP series aims to demystify MRI physics and make it accessible to a broader audience, including students, researchers, MRI technologists (radiographers), clinicians, many professionals whose work indirectly involves MRI (including sales, marketing, legal, regulatory, etc.) , as well as anyone interested in learning about this remarkable technology.

Each year, approximately 100 to 150 million MRI scans are performed worldwide [1]. This means that, on average, about one to two MRI scans being initiated every second somewhere in the world. Given that a typical MRI exam lasts approximately 30 minutes, there are roughly 1,500 to 2,000 MRI scans in progress at any given moment. Most likely, you or someone you know has had an MRI scan, or will have one in the future. Understanding the physics behind MRI enhances our appreciation of this technology and its impact on healthcare.

### Style and Format

The AoMP series will be presented in a visually engaging format, with illustrations and/or animations to help explain complex concepts. Each post will be structured as a challenge/question (**AoMP-xxx-Q**) followed by a detailed solution/answer (**AoMP-xxx-A**), where "xxx" is a three-digit number starting from 000. The three digits "xxx" in the answer post correspond to the same digits in the question post it addresses. For posts that aren't appropriate for challenge format, they will be labeled as **AoMP-xxx**. 

Though the series is primarily focused on MRI physics, it will also cover many general topics in foundational mathematics, physics, and signal processing.

### Why another blog?

First, there are already many excellent resources on MRI physics, including textbooks, YouTube channels, and online courses. However, the resources often lack a comprehensive, visually engaging, and accessible approach that combines accurate explanations with intuitive understanding. 

The AoMP series aims to fill this gap by providing accurate and precise explanations in an engaging and accessible format. The series that I wish I had when I was starting out in MRI physics, and that I hope will be useful to many others as well.

Second, we are living in an age of constant information bombardment fueled by social media. The matter is getting worse with the rapid rise of AI-generated content. Much of what we see is optimized for engagement, not for genuine learning or understanding. It’s easy to get lost in the noise and misled by misinformation.

Online platforms often promote shallow, passive learning over deep, active engagement. Generative AI can further short-circuit the learning process, tempting viewers to use AI for immediate answers without critical thought. The long-term consequences on critical thinking and creativity remain to be seen, but without proper measure, the risks are clear.

To counter this trend, I’ve decided to, when possible, format my posts in form of challenges designed to spark curiosity, encourage creativity, and strengthen critical thinking. I believe in our innate human drive to explore so I hope these challenges foster deeper learning and provide a refreshing alternative to the passive consumption of information that dominates online platforms.
