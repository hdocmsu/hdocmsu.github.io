### What is AoMP?

*The Art of MRI Physics (AoMP)* is a precise and carefully crafted distillation of the author's more than 15 years of experience in MRI physics, signal processing, and mathematics. It is designed to be accessible to readers of all backgrounds. The series covers foundational concepts in mathematics, physics, signal processing, and MRI physics, with an emphasis on intuitive understanding without sacrificing rigor. 

MRI is a life-saving technology that has revolutionized medical imaging and diagnostics, grounded in fascinating physics and mathematics. However, it is often perceived as complex and inaccessible to those without a technical background. The AoMP series aims to demystify MRI physics and make it accessible to a broader audience.

Each year, approximately 100 to 150 million MRI scans are performed worldwide [1]. In other words, on average, about one to two MRI scans being initiated every second somewhere in the world. Given that a typical MRI exam lasts approximately 30 minutes, there are roughly 1,500 to 2,000 MRI scans in progress at any given moment. Most likely, you or someone you know has had an MRI scan, or will have one in the future. Understanding the physics behind MRI can enhance our appreciation of this remarkable technology.

### Style and Format

The AoMP series will be presented in a visually engaging format, using illustrations and/or animations to help explain complex concepts. Each post will be structured as a challenge or question (**AoMP-xxx-Q**) followed by a detailed solution or answer (**AoMP-xxx-A**), where "xxx" is a three-digit number starting from 000. The three digits in the answer post correspond to those in the question it addresses. Posts that are not suitable for the challenge format, will be labeled as **AoMP-xxx**.

### Why another blog?

First, there are already many excellent resources on MRI physics, including textbooks, YouTube channels, and online courses. However, the resources often lack a comprehensive, visually engaging, and accessible approach that combines accuracy with intuitive understanding. 

The AoMP series aims to fill this gap by providing precise explanations in an engaging and accessible format. It is the series I wish I had when I was starting out in MRI physics.

Second, we are living in an age of constant information bombardment fueled by social media. The situation is worsening with the rapid rise of AI-generated content. Much of what we see is optimized for surface engagement rather than genuine. It’s easy to get lost in the noise or misled by misinformation.

Online platforms often promote shallow, passive learning over deep, active engagement. Generative AI can further short-circuit the learning process, tempting viewers to seek immediate answers without critical thought. The long-term consequences for critical thinking and creativity remain to be seen, but without proper safeguards, the risks are evident.

To counter this trend, I’ve chosen, when possible, to structure posts in the form of challenges designed to spark curiosity, encourage creativity, and strengthen critical thinking. I believe in our innate human drive to explore, and I hope these challenges foster deeper learning while offering a refreshing alternative to the passive consumption of information that dominates many online platforms.

### Approach and Philosophy

Over the years, I haven't found a shortcut to achieving deep understanding. It requires time, effort, and active engagement with the material. My learning philosophy is similar to what Richard Feynman expressed in his quote:

> "What I cannot create, I do not understand." 
 
To truly understand a concept, I often re-create it myself, whether through mathematical derivation, or numerical simulation. This process forces me to engage deeply with the material and develop a deep understanding of the underlying principles. Many times, I find that creating something from scratch leads to insights and perspectives that I wouldn't have gained otherwise.

For a focused research direction, Feymann's approach is often the most effective way to achieve deep understanding, enabling original contributions and pushing the boundaries of knowledge. However, this approach can be time-consuming and may not be the most efficient way to learn a broad range of topics, especially for those who are new to the field.

To mitigate that, I propose:

> If you can accurately and intuitively visualize a concept, you can understand it (to an useful extent).

Like Feynman's approach, this method is also time-consuming and not practical for everyone. It requires a certain level of knowledge and skill, as well as the ability to create accurate and intuitive visual representations of complex concepts without dumping them down.

That is the goal of the AoMP series: to provide accurate and intuitive visualizations of complex concepts in MRI physics, mathematics, and signal processing. By doing so, I hope to make these concepts more accessible, easier to understand, and more memorable for a broader audience. Because I believe that:

> Once you see it, you cannot un-see it. It becomes a part of your mental model and can be recalled whenever you need it.


Another Feymann's quote that I find relevant is:

> "The ultimate test of your knowledge is your capacity to convey it to another."

The AoMP series is an attempt to test my ability to convey complex concepts in MRI physics, mathematics, and signal processing to a broader audience, helping them understand and appreciate the beauty of MRI physics. I hope the series will be a valuable resource for anyone interested in learning about MRI physics and that it will inspire others to explore this fascinating field.

I strive to use accurate physical and mathematical modeling for visualization and animation, rather than stitching together eye-catching graphics and moving them around. The goal is to enhance understanding, not merely to make things look appealing.

### Scope of the series

Although the series is primarily focused on MRI physics, it will also cover foundational topics in mathematics, physics, and signal processing. One such topic is Fourier analysis, which is fundamental not only to MRI physics but also to many other fields in science and engineering.

The AoMP series embodies a careful distillation of 15 years of experience in MRI physics, signal processing, and mathematics, much of which reflects perspectives and insights not typically found in textbooks or online resources.

### Target audience

The AoMP series is designed for a broad audience, including students, researchers, MRI technologists (radiographers), clinicians, professionals whose work indirectly involves MRI (including sales, marketing, legal, regulatory, etc.), as well as anyone interested in learning about this remarkable technology.

Note that, the material presented in the AoMP series is not only accessible for broad audiences, including those without prior knowledge, but also valuable to PhD-level MRI physicists and educators, as it reflects what I wish I had access to when I began working in MRI and even during my research and continuing education.
